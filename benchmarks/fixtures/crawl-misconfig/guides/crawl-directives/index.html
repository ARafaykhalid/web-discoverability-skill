Crawl directives and index directives

Two separate mechanisms decide what happens to a URL. One decides whether the URL is fetched at all, and it is stated in robots.txt. The other decides whether the fetched document may be listed, and it is stated in the document itself or in a response header.

The two are often treated as interchangeable, which is where the trouble starts. A rule that forbids fetching a URL also forbids reading anything inside it, including a directive placed there to keep the URL out of the listing. The two instructions look correct side by side and cancel each other in practice, and the state that results is not observable from either file alone.

Reading order matters

Order in the file does not decide the outcome. The longest matching rule wins, and a tie resolves to the permissive side, so a group that forbids everything and then carves exceptions back out is decided by string length rather than by the order someone wrote the lines in.
