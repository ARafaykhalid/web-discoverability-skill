Ferry operator opens consultation on a reduced winter timetable

By Mara Quill. Published 8 May 2026, 07:00. Updated 9 May 2026, 08:30.

Marram Estuary Ferries has opened a six-week consultation on cutting the winter service from seven return sailings a day to four. If the proposal goes through, it would take effect on 1 November and run until the end of March.

The operator's consultation document, published on Friday, keeps the 07:10 from Marram and the 08:05 back from the mainland slip, the pair that carries the school run and most of the commuting. It also keeps the 12:30 and the 16:15. The sailings it proposes to drop are the 09:40, the 14:00, and the 18:40.

The 18:40 is the one that has produced almost all of the response so far. It is the only sailing that gets anyone off a late shift on the mainland back to the village the same evening; without it the next crossing is the following morning. The parish council registered an objection on Saturday, before the consultation had been open two days, and asked the operator to model a four-sailing winter that keeps the 18:40 and drops the 12:30 instead.

"We are not arguing about the number four," the council chair, Delyth Rowan, said. "We are arguing about which four. Cutting the middle of the day is inconvenient. Cutting the end of it takes people's jobs off them."

The operator's case is a crewing one. Winter loadings on the three sailings it wants to drop averaged nine passengers in the last two seasons, against a break-even of twenty-six, and the roster needed to run a seven-sailing day requires a second crew that the company says the winter revenue does not support. Its document puts the annual saving at about £74,000, with roughly £58,000 of that in crew costs.

What the document does not do is show the modelling behind the choice of sailings, and that omission is doing the operator no favours. Asked for it on Friday, the company said the loading figures were commercially sensitive but that it would consider publishing them in aggregate before the consultation closes.

The consultation closes on 19 June. Responses go to the operator, with a copy to the harbour board, which holds the berthing licence and has to be consulted on any timetable that changes the pattern of use at the pontoon. The board's June meeting falls inside the consultation window, on 3 June, and the ferry item is already on the draft agenda.

One practical note for anyone responding: the operator has said it will treat a response as an objection only if it names the sailings it wants retained. A general statement that the service should not be cut will be logged, but it will not be counted against a specific crossing.
