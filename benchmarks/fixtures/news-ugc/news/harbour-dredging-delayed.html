Harbour dredging pushed back to autumn after survey finds silt has shifted

By Mara Quill. Published 11 May 2026, 06:20. Updated 11 May 2026, 17:40.

The dredging of the main channel at Marram, planned for three weeks in June, will not now begin before late September. The harbour board confirmed on Sunday evening that the contractor has released its vessel to another port after a survey found the silt bank in the outer channel is no longer where the dredge plan says it is.

The survey was commissioned in April, after two skippers reported touching bottom on a rising tide at points they had crossed all winter. It found the bank has migrated roughly forty metres east of the position recorded in the 2023 hydrographic plan, and that its crest is about half a metre higher than the figure the plan was costed against. Both numbers matter: the dredge was priced on volume, and the volume has moved.

"You cannot dig where the chart says the mud is when the mud has gone somewhere else," the harbour master, Iwan Pryce, said on Monday. "We would have paid for three weeks of a vessel working the wrong ground, and then paid again in the autumn to work the right ground. I would rather be criticised for the delay than for that."

The board's own papers, published with the agenda for the June meeting, put the cost of the redrawn plan at between eleven and fourteen thousand pounds, most of it a second survey pass in August to confirm the bank has stopped moving. The dredge itself is still budgeted at the figure agreed in February.

The delay has practical consequences for the fishing fleet. Six of the eleven boats working out of Marram draw more than two metres, and their skippers have been keeping to the deep water on the west side of the channel since March. On a spring low that side is workable. On a neap it is a matter of waiting, which for a day boat means losing the tide and often the day.

Tomos Vane, who has fished the estuary for thirty-one years, said the fleet had told the board about the shift long before the survey was commissioned. "We said in January it had moved. We were asked for evidence. We are the evidence. Now there is a piece of paper that says the same thing and it has cost the board a summer."

The ferry is unaffected: its berth is inside the training wall and its draught is under a metre and a half. The pilot boat is also unaffected. The two coasters that call at the grain berth four or five times a year are not, and the board has written to both agents to warn them that arrival windows will be tighter until the work is done.

The next harbour board meeting is on 3 June at the Quay Street offices. The redrawn dredge plan is expected to be tabled there, along with the August survey brief. Papers are usually published a week in advance.
