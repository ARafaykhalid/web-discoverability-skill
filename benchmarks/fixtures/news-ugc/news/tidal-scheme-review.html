Tidal Reach at the Harbour Lights: a review of the estuary play everyone is arguing about

By Mara Quill. Published 6 May 2026, 20:15.

A play about a tidal barrage should not be this funny. Rhian Cadogan's Tidal Reach, which opened at the Harbour Lights on Thursday and runs until the 23rd, spends its first hour on a planning inquiry and is funnier than anything else on this coast this year. Then it spends its second hour explaining itself, and by the interval you can feel the audience deciding which half it came for.

The premise is close enough to home to make the laughter uncomfortable. A barrage is proposed across an estuary that is never named and is obviously this one. Four people give evidence: an engineer who believes the numbers, a fisherman who does not, a councillor who has already decided, and a hydrologist who is right and cannot say so in the four minutes the chair allows her. Cadogan has clearly sat through the real thing. The stage directions for the chair's timer alone are worth the ticket.

Bethan Lloyd-Meyrick's hydrologist is the performance the play is built on. She does the whole first act with a lever-arch file she is not allowed to open, and she makes that file funnier and then sadder than a monologue could have been. When she finally reads from it, in the last ten minutes, the theatre goes properly quiet.

The design is a single scaffold sluice gate that rises through the evening, lit from the wings in a blue that gets colder as the water gets higher. It is a good idea executed once too often; by the fourth cue it has stopped being a metaphor and started being a piece of scenery going up.

The second act is the problem. Having earned its audience with specificity, Cadogan reaches for significance: there is a flood, a chorus, and a long speech about inheritance that would have been better as four lines. Twenty minutes could come out of it without losing an argument, and the director, Osian Trew, is the person best placed to find them.

None of which stops this being the most interesting thing the Harbour Lights has staged since it reopened. It is a local play about a local fight that trusts its audience to know the detail, and the audience repays it. Go for the first act. Stay for Lloyd-Meyrick.

Tidal Reach is at the Harbour Lights until 23 May. Readers have rated the production 3.8 out of five, from 41 ratings submitted on this page.
